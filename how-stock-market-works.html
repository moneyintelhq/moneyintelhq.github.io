How the Stock Market Actually Works

A beginner-friendly explanation of shares, exchanges, buyers, sellers and why prices move.

By Money Intel EditorialSeptember 2026Money Intel

Start with the basic idea

A share represents a small ownership interest in a company. When a company lists shares on a stock exchange, those shares can be bought and sold by investors. The exchange provides the marketplace and rules; brokers provide the access layer that lets investors place orders.

Why does the price change?

At any moment, buyers and sellers have different views about what a share is worth. If buyers are willing to pay more and sellers hold out for higher prices, the market price can rise. If sellers become more willing to accept lower prices, it can fall. News matters because it changes expectations about future cash flows, growth, risk or valuation.

Primary market vs secondary market

In a primary issue, a company raises capital by issuing securities. In the secondary market, investors trade existing securities with one another. Understanding this distinction helps explain why buying a listed share from another investor does not directly send that purchase price to the company.

What beginners should watch

Focus first on the business, not the ticker. Ask how the company makes money, whether demand is durable, whether profits turn into cash, how much debt it carries and what could make the investment thesis wrong. A rising price is not, by itself, evidence that a company is becoming more valuable.

The useful mental model

Think of the stock market as a continuous auction about future expectations. Your job as an investor is not to predict every short-term move. It is to understand the business well enough to judge whether the price offers a sensible relationship between potential return and risk.
