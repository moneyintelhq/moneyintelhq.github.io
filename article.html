How to read an annual report without getting lost in the numbers

A practical framework for separating useful signals from noise.

Money Intel Editorial · September 2026

Start with the business, not the balance sheet.

Before looking at ratios, understand how the company makes money, what drives demand and where management believes the industry is heading. The numbers become easier to interpret once you understand the business.

1. Read the management discussion.

Look for changes in demand, pricing, margins, capital allocation, competition and risks. Compare management's language with what actually happened in the financial statements.

2. Follow the cash.

Revenue and profit tell you about accounting performance. Cash flow tells you whether the business is converting that performance into cash.

3. Build a five-line snapshot.

  • Revenue growth
  • Operating margin
  • Free cash flow
  • ROCE / ROE
  • Debt and liquidity

4. End with questions, not a prediction.

A good research note should leave you with sharper questions: What would invalidate the thesis? Which metric needs monitoring? What does management need to execute well?
