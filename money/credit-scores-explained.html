Credit Scores Explained: What Actually Matters

A plain-English guide to understanding credit scores, repayment behaviour and responsible borrowing.

By Money Intel EditorialSeptember 2026Money Intel

What a credit score is

A credit score is a numerical summary used by lenders as one input when assessing credit risk. It is not a complete picture of your finances and it should not be treated as a measure of your income or wealth.

Payment behaviour matters

Paying dues on time is foundational. Missed or delayed payments can signal higher repayment risk. The practical lesson is simple: borrow only what you can comfortably repay and keep track of every due date.

Keep borrowing intentional

Credit can be useful when it solves a genuine financing need, but repeatedly borrowing to fund routine spending can create a fragile cash-flow situation. A strong score is not a reason to take on more debt.

Think long term

Credit history is built through behaviour over time. Avoiding unnecessary applications, maintaining manageable balances and paying obligations as agreed can support a healthier credit profile.
