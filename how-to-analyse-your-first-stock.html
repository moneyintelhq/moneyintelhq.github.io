How to Analyse Your First Stock

A repeatable framework for researching a company before you decide whether it belongs in your portfolio.

By Money Intel EditorialSeptember 2026Money Intel

1. Understand the business

Write down, in plain language, what the company sells, who buys it, how it makes money and what drives demand. If you cannot explain the business simply, you are not ready to judge the numbers.

2. Study the industry

Look at market size, competition, regulation, pricing power, cyclicality and the company’s position. A good company in a structurally difficult industry can still be a poor investment at the wrong price.

3. Read the financial statements

Track revenue, operating profit, margins, cash flow, debt and return ratios over several years. Avoid focusing on one exceptional year. Trends are usually more informative than isolated figures.

4. Test capital allocation

Ask what management does with the cash the business generates. Reinvestment, acquisitions, dividends, buybacks and debt reduction all have trade-offs. Good capital allocation can compound value; poor allocation can destroy it.

5. Build an investment thesis

Write three parts: why the business can grow or remain resilient, what could go wrong, and what evidence would change your mind. This makes your decision falsifiable instead of emotional.

6. Only then look at valuation

A wonderful company can be a poor investment if expectations are already too high. Compare valuation with growth, profitability, cash generation and risk. Valuation should be the final filter, not the first reason you buy.
